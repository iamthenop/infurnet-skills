# Cross-language phonetics

Match sound, not language identity or spelling. IPA is a shared comparison
notation; broad human pronunciations do not reproduce cat acoustics.

| Class | English | German | Japanese |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meow | meow /miˈaʊ/ | miau /miˈaʊ̯/ | ニャー nyā [ɲaː] |
| Soft meow | mew /mjuː/ | mau /maʊ̯/ | にゃん nyan [ɲaɴ] |
| Purr | purr /pɝ/ | schnurr /ʃnʊʁ/ | ゴロゴロ gorogoro [ɡoɾoɡoɾo] |
| Hiss | hiss /hɪs/ | fauch /faʊ̯x/ | シャー shā [ɕaː] |
| Low call | grrr (variable) | knurr /knʊʁ/ | グルル gururu [ɡɯɾɯɾɯ] |

Words naming the same kind of sound are not automatically similar in IPA.
For example, *purr* and *schnurr* are semantically aligned but
phonetically different. Likewise, にゃー is not a transcription of *miau*.

Illustrative candidates, never mandatory substitutions:

- perfect → purr-fect; personal → purr-sonal.
- now → meow or miau; compare the two forms phonetically rather than
  preferring the English spelling.
- じゃない (janai) → じゃにゃい (janyai).
- なるほど (naruhodo) → にゃるほど (nyaruhodo).
- Missverständnis → Miezverständnis; this is primarily visual wordplay
  and must still pass the phonetic gate or a conventional exception.

Feline sounds belong to language-neutral repetition families such as
\`meow\`, \`purr\`, \`hiss\`, \`trill\`, and \`chatter\`. The spellings
*meow* and *miau* share the meowing family; their spelling difference
does not create a new repetition allowance.

If no eligible replacement preserves natural speech, make no change.
