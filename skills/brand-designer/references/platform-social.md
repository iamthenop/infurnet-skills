# Reference: Social Platform

Background for filling out `assets/brand-social-template.md`.

## Why this platform needs its own doc

Social is the one medium where the brand doesn't control the canvas — the platform does. Fixed avatar crops, story UI overlays, and auto-generated thumbnails will clip or obscure content that isn't designed with those constraints in mind from the start. A brand doc for social has to document platform *constraints*, not just brand preferences.

## What separates a lightweight guide from a mature one

- **Safe zones are platform UI, not brand style.** Instagram Stories cover roughly the top ~14% and bottom ~20% of the canvas with platform chrome (profile info, reply bar) that the brand doc author doesn't control and that changes over time as platforms update their UI. Document current safe zones, but flag them as "verify against current platform specs" rather than treating them as permanent brand rules — they drift with platform redesigns in a way core brand values never do.
- **Avatar crops are circular on most platforms; source logos are usually square/rectangular.** A logo lockup that looks fine in a square preview can lose its icon entirely once cropped to a circle. This is why the template calls for testing the mark against the actual crop, not just the source file.
- **Register varies by platform even when voice doesn't.** The core doc's voice attributes (e.g. "confident, warm, direct") stay constant, but how formal, how terse, or how much humor is appropriate shifts by platform norm — LinkedIn audiences expect a different register than TikTok audiences, even from the identical brand personality. This is a common point of confusion: teams sometimes think a "more casual" Instagram voice means the brand's core voice changed, when really it's the same voice adapted to a different room.
- **Thumbnails and captions are accessibility surfaces, not afterthoughts.** Alt text, burned-in captions, and sufficient contrast on text-over-photo graphics all belong here because social is often where a brand's content reaches the widest and most varied audience — including screen-reader users and people watching muted video in public.

## Common mistakes to flag when drafting or auditing

- Designing only for the square/1:1 canvas and assuming platform auto-crop will handle the rest — it usually clips important content.
- Treating "safe zone" percentages as fixed forever — note that these should be spot-checked against current platform specs periodically, since platforms redesign their UI.
- Conflating voice (constant) with register (platform-dependent) — don't let a "make Instagram more fun" request quietly change the brand's actual voice attributes in the core doc.
- No alt-text or caption requirement documented — an accessibility gap that's easy to miss because it's invisible to most viewers.

## When to go deeper than the template

If the brand runs paid social at scale, recommend a companion doc (outside this skill's scope) covering ad-specific specs — safe zones and aspect ratios for paid formats often differ from organic post specs on the same platform.
